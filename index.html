Operations Manual — Unit 1 Startup

You are the main control room operator. Take the plant from an open, defueled reactor all the way to 100% power on the grid. Use the time-acceleration buttons (up to 900x) for the slow evolutions, and slow back down for the delicate ones (criticality, draws, syncing). Space bar pauses; SCRAM shuts it down. Pick a difficulty from the LEVEL button: Beginner widens every acceptance band, softens the pressurizer, relaxes the trips and shortens the holds; Real Simulation is the full-fidelity plant. A reactor trip costs points (150 real / 40 beginner).

How the plant fits together — what drives what

Spend a minute on this; every phase below is just a subset of it.

  • Reactivity → reactor power. Power rises when total reactivity is positive, falls when it is negative. You add reactivity by withdrawing rods, by diluting boron (DILUTE removes boron; BORATE adds it, about −10 pcm/ppm), and the plant removes it for you through temperature feedback: hotter coolant and fuel both push reactivity down. That negative feedback is what makes the reactor follow steam demand.
  • RCP shaft heat → temperature (cold plant). Below criticality your only heat source is the reactor coolant pumps — each adds about 5.9 MW, so pump count sets the heatup rate (1 pump ≈ 9 C/h up to 4 pumps ≈ 52 C/h).
  • Nuclear heat vs. steam draw → Tavg (hot plant). Once critical, reactor heat raises Tavg; the steam dump and the turbine remove heat and lower it. Steady Tavg means heat in = heat out.
  • Pressurizer temperature → RCS pressure (with a bubble). With a steam bubble, pressure equals the saturation pressure of the pressurizer water: heaters raise it, spray lowers it. Water-solid (no bubble), pressure is stiff and jumps with any net volume change, so you trim it with charging vs. letdown.
  • Charging − letdown → inventory. Net positive fills the RCS (level, and when solid pressure, go up); net negative drains it. Charging also carries the boron you select.
  • Feedwater vs. steam flow → SG level; SG temperature → SG pressure. Feed more than you steam and level rises; a hotter SG runs at higher pressure, and the turbine can only pull rated steam from a hot, high-pressure SG.

The P-T “chaussette” diagram (always visible, top-right) plots your operating point against the allowed envelope — keep the dot inside the coloured domains. The Plant Data readouts and the annunciator tell you the rest.

Phase 1 — Refueling

  • Load the core, verify rods inserted and boron ≥ 2000 ppm, install the vessel head, then fill & vent the RCS. The plant is now water-solid (no steam bubble anywhere, pressure reacts sharply to volume changes).

Phase 2 — Heatup & pressurization

  • Raise pressure to ~30 bar with charging > letdown, then start the 4 RCPs (they need ≥ 24 bar and trip below ~20 bar).
  • Energize pressurizer heaters. While solid, thermal expansion drives pressure up fast — bleed with letdown to stay in the green domain of the P-T diagram ("chaussette", right-hand panel): max 31 bar until 177 °C.
  • When PRZ temperature > ~230 °C, drain (letdown > charging) to draw a steam bubble. Pressure then follows PRZ saturation temperature: heaters raise it, spray lowers it.
  • The reactor coolant pumps are your heat source. Each running pump dissipates its ~5.9 MW of shaft power straight into the primary water, so pump count sets the heatup rate: 1 pump ≈ 9 °C/h, 4 pumps ≈ 52 °C/h. Stay under the 56 °C/h fatigue limit (HEATUP indicator / HEATUP RATE HI alarm) — run fewer pumps for a gentler ramp, all four once you want to get hot. Climb the blue sock of the P-T diagram: above 177 °C raise pressure with temperature, staying between the upper (overpressure) and lower (saturation margin) boundaries — alarms P-T ENV HIGH/LOW sound if you leave the envelope, and sustained violations cost points. Manage the level swell with letdown (target 40–70%).
  • For pressure, fine balance matters: the CVCS charging and letdown sliders step in 0.1 kg/s and have − / + buttons (hold to repeat). The CVCS panel shows the net flow; a small positive net raises pressure/level, a small negative net lowers them.
  • Finish at 155 bar / 291 °C (PRZ at ~345 °C does this).

Phase 3 — Approach to criticality

  • Withdraw shutdown banks fully, then the control bank to ~60%.
  • Select DILUTE with good charging flow and watch source-range counts climb. Near criticality the startup rate (SUR) rises — go to low time acceleration, stop diluting, and control with rods. Trip setpoint: 5 dpm.
  • Stabilize between 1 kW and 30 MW with |SUR| < 0.3 dpm.

Phase 4 — Power ascension & turbine

  • Withdraw rods to climb above 8% power. Nuclear heat now dominates: open the steam dump to hold Tavg ≈ 292 °C, and start feedwater gently (cold aux feed overcools the SG if you slam it in).
  • Latch & roll the turbine (needs SG ≥ 60 bar), sync the breaker at 3000 rpm, then raise load to ~300 MWe and close the dump. The reactor follows steam demand through the negative moderator coefficient.

Phase 5 — Ramp to 100%

  • Raise the load setpoint stepwise to 1100 MWe. Keep Tavg on the program (no-load 291 °C rising to 306.5 °C by 60% load, then flat — see T REF indicator) by withdrawing rods and diluting boron. Match feedwater to steam flow, keep SG level 40–60% and all 4 RCPs running.

Phase 6 — Steady state

  • Hold ≥ 1078 MWe with every parameter in limits for the hold time (5 minutes real / 90 seconds beginner) to finish.

Quick controls reference

  • Reactivity: control bank & shutdown banks (IN/HOLD/OUT), CVCS makeup (BORATE/NORMAL/DILUTE), RESET RX TRIP.
  • Pressurizer: proportional + backup heaters (pressure up), spray (pressure down), PORV indication.
  • CVCS: charging & letdown flow — sets inventory, water-solid pressure and boron transport.
  • RCPs: 4 coolant pumps — heat source and flow; need ≥ 24 bar to run.
  • Secondary: steam dump (Tavg control), feedwater (SG level).
  • Turbine-generator: latch & roll, sync breaker, load setpoint.

Trips to avoid

High neutron flux (118% Pn), high startup rate (5 dpm real / 9 beginner), PRZ pressure < 131 / > 169 bar (safety valves lift at 172), core-outlet temperature > 330 °C, SG level < 15%, fewer than 4 RCPs above 30% power, and low PRZ level at power. Beginner raises the startup-rate trip and softens the score penalties, but the protective logic is the same plant.
